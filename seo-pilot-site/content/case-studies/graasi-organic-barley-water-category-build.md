## Challenge

"Organic barley water" wasn't really a US category — fewer than 1,000 searches a month. Graasi had a great-tasting, USDA-certified product but no audience asking for it by name. The job was to create demand, not capture it.

## Strategy

### Adjacent-category capture

Targeted higher-volume parent queries — "wellness drinks", "low-calorie hydration", "antioxidant beverages" — and used them to introduce barley water as the answer.

### Health-claim content backed by sourcing

"Barley water benefits", "is barley water keto", "barley water vs coconut water" — each grounded in primary sources so AI engines would quote it confidently.

### Retailer co-marketing SEO

Built location-aware "where to buy Graasi" pages that doubled as SEO landings AND retailer-relationship content.

## Result

- **5.2x** category share-of-voice (Graasi vs. all "barley water" results)
- Top-5 for all **11** primary barley-water queries
- **180%** lift in branded search volume — people now Google "Graasi" by name
- **41%** increase in DTC reorder rate from informed buyers

## Purpose

For new categories, SEO isn't about capturing demand — it's about creating it through adjacent-query education. Graasi shows the playbook.
